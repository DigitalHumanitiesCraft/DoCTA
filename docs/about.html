DoCTA

    About

    Project

    DoCTA (Doing Court in the Tyrolean Alps) studies court practices at the court of Sigismund of Tyrol (1427–1496) from account books, inventories, copybooks and court ordinances held by the Tyrolean State Archives. This site is the digital component of the project: an agentic edition pipeline that turns the Transkribus facsimiles into reliable research data and a digital edition. The digital tools are heuristic instruments for the historical research questions; the goal is to open up different source types for joint analysis.

    Project lead: University of Salzburg. Digital implementation: Digital Humanities Craft OG. The development path is documented as a Promptotyping case study in the Knowledge Vault.

    Usage

    • The home page lists all sources with search and filters; sources with full text open the facsimile–transcription view in the viewer.
    • The viewer offers a facsimile–transcription synopsis and a reading mode over the whole text, and scholarly review and correction take place there.
    • The benchmark documents the quality of the VLM transcription across versioned prompt iterations.
    • The exploration shows the content layer (persons, places, objects) extracted from the sources.
    • The Knowledge Vault holds the Promptotyping documents behind the project: requirements, data, decisions, evaluation protocol.

    Data sources and rights

    Prosopographic data: SiCProD (Austrian Academy of Sciences). Several inventories have already been edited and published by the Inventaria project on Transkribus Sites; where their published transcriptions appear here, they are used with attribution to Inventaria. Facsimiles and further transcriptions: Transkribus (READ-COOP). The historical source material is property of the Tyrolean State Archives; IIIF images are served by Transkribus.
