DoCTA

    About

    Project

    DoCTA (Doing Court in the Tyrolean Alps) studies court practices at the court of Sigismund of Tyrol (1427–1496) from account books, inventories, copybooks and court ordinances held by the Tyrolean State Archives. This site is the digital component of the project: an agentic edition pipeline that turns the Transkribus facsimiles into reliable research data and a digital edition. The digital tools are heuristic instruments for the historical research questions; the goal is to open up different source types for joint analysis.

    Project lead: University of Salzburg. Digital implementation: Digital Humanities Craft OG. The development path is documented as a Promptotyping case study in the Knowledge Vault.

    What this prototype demonstrates

    1. Methodical feasibility on the actual source material. The pipeline runs end to end on the Transkribus collection of the Tyrolean State Archives, from facsimile to validated TEI and aggregated research data. Every artifact on this site is derived from that collection; nothing is mocked.
    2. The methods work on Early New High German manuscripts. Vision-language models transcribe fifteenth-century Bastarda and Kurrent directly from the page images, and the benchmark measures how well, against scholarly reference transcriptions.
    3. How far current models go without any training. No model is fine-tuned. Transcription quality is steered through versioned, frozen prompt iterations alone, and each iteration is measured under repetition so that progress is attributable to the prompt rather than to run-to-run noise.
    4. How annotation and revision will proceed in practice. The viewer contains the working review loop: a scholar records page decisions and line corrections in the browser, the export is ingested into the page register with full provenance, and corrected readings flow into the TEI. Extracted entities are anchored to lines, gathered in a corpus-wide register and published as a JSON-LD graph for exploration. All machine output is marked unrevised until a scholar approves it.

    The pipeline

    1. Facsimiles and existing transcriptions come from Transkribus (IIIF); a subset of the inventories carries transcriptions by the Inventaria project, named as such wherever they appear.
    2. Vision-language models transcribe pages against versioned, frozen prompts; runs are stored append-only with full provenance (prompt version and hash, model, parameters) and are never overwritten.
    3. A page register records every page with its content class, verification status and all transcription runs; a better transcription is a new run, never an edit.
    4. Scholarly review happens in the viewer; exports are validated as a whole before anything is written, so a stale or inconsistent batch changes nothing.
    5. The TEI edition is generated deterministically from register and runs, one file per document plus a corpus-wide entity register, and validated in two stages: TEI conformance and a closed project grammar in which certainty attributes are impossible by construction.
    6. The entity layer is aggregated into a JSON-LD graph that records, for every node, where it is attested and which step (model or deterministic workflow) produced it.

    Builders read no clock and make no network calls, so every rebuild is byte-identical; a healthcheck verifies this together with cross-artifact consistency on every commit.

    Usage guide

    • Find a source. The home page lists all sources with search, filters and processing stage; sources with full text open in the viewer.
    • Read a source. The viewer offers a facsimile–transcription synopsis with line highlighting, a reading mode over the whole text, the TEI encoding with download, and adjustable text size. Chips above the text name the text layer (human-corrected or machine) and its origin.
    • Review a source. The Review button starts the correction workflow: reviewer initials, a decision per page, corrections per line, exported as JSON for the pipeline.
    • Judge the transcription quality. The benchmark compares prompt iterations on a fixed page set, with links from every measured page to its facsimile and text.
    • Explore the content layer. The exploration shows extracted persons, places and objects as a network over the sources and as a sortable table, with provenance on every record.
    • Understand the decisions. The Knowledge Vault holds the Promptotyping documents behind the project: requirements, data, evaluation protocol, design decisions and the decision journal.

    Data sources and rights

    Prosopographic data: SiCProD (Austrian Academy of Sciences). Several inventories have already been edited and published by the Inventaria project on Transkribus Sites; where their published transcriptions appear here, they are used with attribution to Inventaria. Facsimiles and further transcriptions: Transkribus (READ-COOP). The historical source material is property of the Tyrolean State Archives; IIIF images are served by Transkribus.
